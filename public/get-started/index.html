Get Started with Web Historia

Free and open source. The game runs entirely in your browser, with an AI provider you choose — here is how to get playing in a few minutes.

Play in Your Browser

Nothing to install

Press Play and Web Historia loads right in your tab. There is no desktop app and no download — the world map streams from the community content-node network, verified byte-for-byte by checksum, and your games and scenarios are saved in your browser.

Signing in is optional; it syncs your games and scenarios between devices, encrypted before they leave your browser. Your AI key goes straight from your browser to your provider — never through a Web Historia server.

Bring your own AI

Web Historia has no scripted simulation: every time skip, every event, every border change and every leader's reply is generated by a language model. Without a connected AI provider the world cannot advance — you can look at the map and use the map editor, but not play.

Open a game, open Settings, and paste an OpenRouter API key (recommended — one key unlocks curated model suggestions; leave the model blank for the auto-router). Gemini, OpenAI, Anthropic, and OpenAI-compatible local models through Ollama or LM Studio work too. The key is stored in your browser and sent only to the provider you chose. See the AI setup guide for every option.

Your First Game

Step 1 — The main menu

The menu has three tabs: Games (your saves), Scenarios (the worlds you can start from, with their editor), and Community (the Scenario Hub).

Step 2 — New Game

Click New Game. Modern Day (starting 1 January 2016) is the built-in scenario; era presets like World War II — 1939 and Medieval — 1200 AD can be imported from the Community tab first. Click the country you want to lead on the picker map, then choose a difficulty from Very Easy to Impossible.

Step 3 — Look around

Click any country on the world map. Its panel shows what the map-maker wrote about it, an AI intelligence briefing on request, and an Open Diplomacy button that starts a chat with its leader.

Step 4 — Act

Type what your nation does in the Actions panel in plain language — mobilise, sign a treaty, fund a programme, move troops. Actions queue up and are resolved at the next time skip. Negotiate with other leaders in chat, ask the Advisor for a briefing or a plan, and deploy spies from the chat panel.

Step 5 — Skip time

Click the date at the top of the screen and choose how far to jump — from 6 hours to 1 year. The AI resolves your queued actions, plays every other nation, writes the events of that period and moves borders and fronts. Read the events on the timeline, then act again.

Good to Know

  • Single-player: you lead one nation and the AI plays the rest. There is no multiplayer lobby.
  • Saves: the game saves itself as you play, in your browser. Sign in to sync between devices.
  • Scenario Hub: browse and import community scenarios from the Community tab, and publish your own.
  • Map editor: every scenario opens in a full vector map editor — redraw borders, paint owners, place cities, then Apply & Play.

Web Historia is an AI-driven turn-based strategy game that runs entirely in your browser. Lead any nation on a living world map, act in plain language, and watch an AI simulate the consequences — wars, diplomacy, economies, and borders that actually shift.

▶ Play in your browser 📜 How it works

Nothing to install. You bring your own AI key (one from OpenRouter), it goes straight from your browser to the provider — never through us.

Up and running in minutes

Three steps to play

No accounts, no store, no build tools to wrangle. Open the game, connect an AI, and start reshaping history.

I

Open the game

Click Play and Web Historia loads right in your tab — there is no desktop app and nothing to download. Your games save to your browser.

II

Connect your AI

Paste a free OpenRouter key in Settings. Leave the model blank for the recommended auto-router, or pick a curated model per task.

III

Play

Choose a scenario — Modern Day, WWII 1939, Medieval 1200, Bronze Age 1200 BC and more — pick a country, and write the first event of your timeline.

Eras, not eras alone

Any century, any world

Unlike single-era grand strategy games, Web Historia is built around presets — every era is a map, a prompt pack, and simulation rules. Play the official ones, import community worlds from the in-game Scenario Hub, or build your own.

Modern Day · built in World War II — 1939 Medieval — 1200 AD Rome — 117 AD Mongol World — 1300 AD New World — 1650 Bronze Age — 1200 BC

Making your own preset is the same tool the game is built on: rearrange the map in the built-in editor, write the prompt and rules the AI follows, and share the bundle on the hub.

Grow the network

Host a server node

Run a small node on your own device to help players load the world map faster. It's deliberately safe: a node only ever serves read-only map data, verified byte-for-byte by checksum. It never touches anyone's games, accounts, or AI keys, and never runs their code — so it can't be turned against players.

I

Install in one click

Download the node and run install.bat (Windows) or install.sh (macOS/Linux). It sets everything up and downloads the verified map content for you.

II

Put it online

Expose it over HTTPS — a free Cloudflare Tunnel is easiest (no open ports, hides your IP). The README walks you through it step by step.

III

You're live

Your node registers itself and starts serving straight away — no waiting, no extra steps on your side. Players only ever use nodes listed in the project's signed directory, and an admin can pause or remove any node from it at any time.

Good to know

Questions & requirements

Do I need to install anything?

No. Web Historia runs in your browser — games, saves, and scenarios live there (IndexedDB), with sign-in syncing them across devices. The only thing you bring is an AI API key.

Do I need an AI key to play?

Yes — the AI is the simulation. Every time skip, event, and diplomatic reply is generated by a language model, so the world cannot advance without one. A free OpenRouter key unlocks curated recommended models; leave the model field blank and OpenRouter's auto-router picks the best one per request.

Is it really free?

Yes. Web Historia is free and open source under the GNU AGPL v3, forked from Open Historia. You only pay your AI provider for the tokens the game actually uses — bring your own key and you control the spend.

Where does my data live?

In your browser. Saves and scenarios are stored locally (IndexedDB) and sync when you sign in; your AI key is stored in your browser too and is sent only to the provider you chose — never through a Web Historia server.

Can I make my own scenario?

Yes — that's a core feature. Any scenario is a map, a prompt pack, and simulation rules: rearrange the world in the built-in map editor, write the instructions the AI follows, and export a bundle to share on the Scenario Hub.

Can I help?

Yes! Report issues and star the GitHub repo, publish scenarios to the hub, or run a content node to speed up map loading for everyone.
